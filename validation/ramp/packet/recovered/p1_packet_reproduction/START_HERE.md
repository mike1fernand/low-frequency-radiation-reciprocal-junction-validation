# Paper One packet reproduction and mathematical check
Research note, November 1, 2026, Toronto.

## Result

The packet is reproducible in Paper One’s declared free scalar line and in its finite coupled line and vector bulk. The previous reproduction limit applies to a different candidate for Paper Three, a row of identical nodes embedded throughout the homogeneous three-dimensional bulk. One minor domain clarification was found in the stationary packet-average expression. It does not change the time simulation or explain the ordinary-row result.

## Checks made

- The unit-mass free scalar chain has stiffness 1.69 and force 1.69(qⱼ₊₁−2qⱼ+qⱼ₋₁). Inserting an axial traveling harmonic gives ω=2.6|sin(k/2)| for unit spacing. At the reference carrier k₀=2 asin(6/13)=0.95945727535…, it gives ω=1.2 and positive group velocity 1.15325625947…. The real-valued velocity initialization chooses the conjugate traveling phases consistently. The carrier at the center of a Gaussian envelope need not be a discrete Fourier bin.
- The independent scalar reconstruction gives 0.99999974231265 of periodic diagnostic energy in right-moving components. The stationary Nyquist fraction is 0.00000025768735. Both numbers and the 0.999683492758 coverage of the 14 distinct in-band frequencies agree with SM5C. Free open-line energy is about 3.59729783033 × 10⁻⁵; the periodic closing bond adds only about 9.184 × 10⁻⁹ in relative terms. Periodic Fourier propagation, diagonalization of the independently assembled real stiffness and real-space time integration agree through the sampled structure times up to 54 in their respective boundary models.
- For the full simulation, the Fourier transform initializes the 120 velocities, then the 120-site scalar line and 120×56×56 bulk evolve with free ends and no wrap bonds. The 48 node-to-node contact links occupy segments 60–65. Velocity Verlet uses step 0.04. Two exact transverse reflection symmetries can be used for evolution; the complete grid is reconstructed before measuring the open energies and periodic modal content. The accepted reporting interval is recovered from the five predicates at cadence 0.2. The comparison uses all 14 stored snapshots within the accepted interval. It includes both limiting allocations inside degenerate eigenspaces, and divides by the open initial scalar energy.
- The full-device result reproduces the manuscript’s window [52.8,55.4] and the reported lower-branch fraction 0.1276263869, about 12.76% of the original scalar packet energy. The independently reconstructed midpoint was 0.12762638687658; SM5C Eq. (75) prints 0.12762638690945. The difference is 3.29 × 10⁻¹¹ of initial energy. Its exact last-digit origin is unresolved. The displayed allocation intervals overlap, and neither is a bound on all integrator or arithmetic errors. SM5C’s stated current-balance residual, 0.0296799%, also reproduces. The 132 upper–middle doublets and 231 triple degeneracies are recovered. This is a finite-time stored-energy diagnostic, distinct from the monochromatic incident-power coefficient and from the much larger lower-branch share of bulk energy alone.
- On [0,20], comparing the loaded open device directly with the free periodic scalar packet gives about 0.338% RMS difference in the moving part of the whole row velocity (trapezoidal integration at the production step). The separate Paper Three test gives a lower bound above 80.6% in the infinite-transverse, periodic-axial, freely evolving homogeneous harmonic graph at equal energy. The time quadrature and graph differ, so the two percentages are not subtracted or used as a proof of one another.
- Independent summation of actual contact link energies and individual endpoint forces agrees with the reduced Hamiltonian, reciprocal force and half-link energy-current identities. A simple mechanical control copies the packet to a bulk row with all other bulk sites initially at rest. Its axial acceleration away from the contact is then Δq−4q, from the central axial and face-diagonal links. At site 18 it gives −0.00972745 compared with −0.00291939 on the scalar line. Even merely copying this state requires 2.1753 times the scalar diagnostic energy. That last factor is for that specific initialization at one instant. The earlier optimized finite-time bound allows arbitrary initial positions and velocities of surrounding nodes.

## Clarification for Eq. (151)

The unit-flux coefficient is defined on the propagating interval. The shortest-wavelength (Nyquist) DFT component can store energy, but its group velocity and incident flux are zero. Eq. (151) writes the stationary packet numerator as a sum over all bins without separately defining its coefficient at the endpoint. SM5C identifies the component and its small spectral weight. The fourteen evaluated frequencies avoid the endpoint and the coefficient does not enter the direct time evolution.

A precise moving-energy convention that preserves the present denominator is to write the numerator as a sum over bins of nonzero incident group velocity and positive frequency; use every bin in the denominator. Alternatively define an appropriate band-edge coefficient explicitly. Any passive endpoint assignment between 0 and 1 can alter the full normalized average by at most 2.577 × 10⁻⁷, or about 0.0000258 percentage points. With the moving-only convention the upper bound in (152) is conservative. The finite-time result (150) and computed in-band stationary contribution are unaffected.

## Reproduction and scope

See free_line/ for the scalar bin, propagation and energy checks, geometry/ for the pair-force and single-row controls, and device/ for the finite coupled-device solver, reporting checks and output. Numerical agreement in packet preparation and evolution establishes reproducibility to the precision reported in this audit. This is a focused audit of the packet, coupling, energy definitions and their comparison; it does not certify every theorem, all-frequency quadrature, or the physical identification of a scalar line in Discrete Space Theory.

From the extracted folder, run `python free_line/check.py`, `python geometry/contact_check.py`, `python geometry/row_control.py`, and `python device/replay.py`. The last command independently builds and runs the production-sized device in a temporary directory and checks its result. Python 3.11+, NumPy 2.x, SciPy and a C++17 compiler with OpenMP are needed. A method reference for the monatomic chain and its dispersion is [Cambridge, Lattice dynamics and phonons](https://www.sp.phy.cam.ac.uk/files/media/qcmp_lectures_5-8_2020-21.pdf).
