            Generation of Three-Dimensional Quasi-Transverse Elastic Waves from a
                One-Dimensional Longitudinal Channel in a Harmonic Lattice
                                                        M. Fernandes
                                                   Independent Researcher
                                                 (Dated: September 21, 2026)
                We study how a finite junction transfers energy from a prepared longitudinal line wave into a
              three-dimensional elastic medium. A scalar axial channel is coupled to a vector harmonic lattice by
              passive paired central-force springs, with no incident bulk wave. The paired geometry produces axial
              and radial source patterns whose forces and reciprocal response follow from one Hamiltonian. At
              the dimensionless reference frequency ω = 1.2, approximately 12.6% of the incident power enters
              the separated lower acoustic cluster, identified as quasi-transverse (qT); this is about 91.9% of the
              radiated bulk power. An exact centered source-sector reduction retains the infinite exterior through
              a projected Green matrix. Separately implemented frequency-domain formulations corroborate the
              reference coefficient. Direct packet evolution from zero bulk displacement and velocity supplies
              further numerical corroboration when compared with the frequency-domain response using the
              packet’s energy spectrum. Source controls and a decomposition of the full state show that axial and
              radial patterns affect the modal power through their combined response and interference. A certified
              bound retains more than 10.3% incident-qT conversion throughout simultaneous ±5% changes of
              the six segment couplings at the reference frequency. The result connects local source geometry to
              incident-normalized elastic radiation in a specified finite reciprocal junction.
